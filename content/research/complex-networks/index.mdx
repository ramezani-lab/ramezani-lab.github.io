The structure of a social network is rarely available, but the traces left by
information moving through it usually are. That asymmetry drives a family of
problems the lab has worked on for years: inferring the underlying network from
observed cascades while preserving its real topological properties, recovering
what was missing when the data is only partially observed, detecting communities
from diffusion rather than links, and predicting early which cascades will grow.

This line of work produced DANI and DiffStru, and continues through
survival-analysis models for early popularity prediction.
