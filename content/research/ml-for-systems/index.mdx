Database engines are full of decisions made by hand-written heuristics: which
join order to pick, what to index, what to keep in memory, how to set hundreds
of configuration knobs. Each of those is a prediction problem with a measurable
cost, which makes them natural targets for learning.

Our interest is in learned components that survive production: models that
adapt as workloads drift, that degrade gracefully when their predictions are
wrong, and whose training cost is small enough to be worth paying. We study
learned query optimization and cost estimation, workload-aware indexing and
caching, and automatic configuration tuning.
